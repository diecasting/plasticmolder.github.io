## Industry Context Shapes the Spec

The same plastic part means different things in different industries. An automotive connector must survive under-hood heat and a line-down event at the plant; a medical component must hold biocompatibility and traceability; a consumer electronics housing must balance appearance, feel, and cost. The industry sets the bar, and the molding program is built to clear it.

That is why our industry pages describe the documentation and controls each market expects, not just the process.

## Industry Programs

- **Automotive** — PPAP documentation, traceability, and 50T–800T production. See [automotive injection molding](/en/automotive-injection-molding/).
- **Medical** — ISO 13485-controlled molding for devices and components. See [medical plastic injection molding](/en/medical-plastic-injection-molding/) and [medical device components](/en/medical-device-components-molding/).
- **Industrial** — durable, high-volume components. See [industrial plastic injection molding](/en/industrial-plastic-injection-molding/).
- **Consumer Electronics** — tight-tolerance, cosmetic housings and connectors. See [consumer electronics injection molding](/en/consumer-electronics-injection-molding/).
- **EV & Energy** — flame-retardant, traceable battery and charging parts. See [EV and battery plastic components](/en/ev-battery-components-molding/).

## One Facility, Many Markets

Because tooling, molding, and secondary operations share a building, the same quality system serves every industry — only the documentation and process window change. That is what lets a single supplier support a program from prototype through regulated high volume.
