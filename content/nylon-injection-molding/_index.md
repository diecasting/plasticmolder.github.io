## Nylon (PA6 / PA66) Injection Molding at Production Scale

Nylon injection molding covers the workhorse engineering plastics behind most gears, bushings, and structural clips. PA6 and PA66 give teams a rare combination of toughness, fatigue resistance, and low cost that few materials match.

The catch with nylon is moisture. It absorbs water from the air, which changes dimensions and properties after molding. A sound program controls drying, regrind, and conditioning so the part that ships matches the part that was qualified.

## What the Capability Numbers Mean for Your Program

The specifications define the envelope in which nylon (pa6 / pa66) injection molding stays predictable. Tolerances of ±0.03 mm describe where critical features are controlled rather than left to chance.

Tooling lead time of 20 - 40 days reflects complexity and validation, and an annual capacity above 40,000,000 parts signals a floor built for sustained volume.

## How a Program Runs

1. **Design for manufacturability.** Geometry, gate location, and draft are reviewed so the part molds predictably and meets function.
2. **Material and tool selection.** Resin grade and tool steel are chosen for the performance, volume, and life the program requires.
3. **Tool fabrication.** The mold is machined, with hot-runner or multi-cavity features where the program specifies them.
4. **Molding and process setup.** Parameters are developed and locked so fill, pack, and cooling stay inside the validated window.
5. **In-process verification.** Critical dimensions are checked with gauges and CMM; results are recorded for traceability.
6. **Packaging and shipment.** Parts are inspected, packed, and shipped against the agreed quality and delivery plan.

## Material and Application Fit

Material pairing with glass fill or impact modifiers is selected against the load and temperature the part sees. We validate shrinkage and warpage on tryout and lock process windows so high-volume nylon runs stay within tolerance.
